1. The 25-field dictionary

One record is one visa product for one combination of passport, destination, travel purpose and travel document type. A route with several products yields several records. The export and the online display use the same field set, plus one extra column that refines the top-level requirement class. Twenty fields are required and five are provided if available, matching the requirements specification.

2. The three kinds of blank

A blank cell is never ambiguous. Every unfilled cell belongs to exactly one of three kinds, and its reason is stated on screen and in the export:

KindDisplayCounted in the completeness denominator
Not published"Not publicly available". The official page was checked and publishes no value for this item.Excluded from the fillable caliber
Does not apply"Does not apply to this route". For example, a visa-free route has no visa validity.Excluded from the fillable caliber
Missing"missing". Research not yet complete. This is open work.Counted, and lowers completeness

3. Completeness calibers

The acceptance documents state completeness in two ways. Both are computed and shown side by side on the console's completeness tile.

First, the cell caliber of section 6.1: filled cells divided by required cells. The console shows the fillable reading as the headline (the denominator excludes not-published and does-not-apply cells, and the number excluded is shown so it can be audited) alongside the literal reading.

Second, the record caliber of section 4.2.2: records with all 25 fields filled divided by all records.

The gap between the two is structural. A perfectly correct visa-free record can never fill all 25 fields, because fee and validity do not apply to it, and section 4.2.1 prohibits inventing what no government publishes. Which caliber governs acceptance is a decision for the joint meeting.

4. The confidence ladder

GradeRule (per 4.2.3)Handling
HighA person verified the record against a named official page; the record is complete with no conflicts.Shown
MediumBacked by an official source, with a few fields still to fill, or fields the government does not publish.Shown, with the source attached
LowSources conflict, or only non-official sources exist, or a specific product and fee is claimed without a read of the official page.Held. Readers see "We are checking this route" until an operator confirms it

Grading is automatic. The only ways out of a hold are writing a fact verified against an official source, or an audited operator release. Both leave a record.

5. Calibers awaiting a joint ruling

Three calibers are declared honestly in the data and need the joint meeting's ruling:

First, info_validity. The dictionary defines it as "the date the policy itself is valid until", but most governments publish no end date. Current caliber: where a policy publishes an end date, that date is used (for example the Korea group exemption to 31 December 2026); otherwise the value is the date until which Ellis guarantees the answer, which is the date the freshness monitor requires a re-check. The export declares this.

Second, the fourth value of visa_requirement, Conditional. About 146 records genuinely depend on the traveler's own circumstances, for example visa-free for holders of a valid third-country visa. They can be folded into the "visa-free, conditional" detail; the migration script is ready and runs on confirmation.

Third, the completeness caliber, see section 3.

6. Special policy notes

Regional and transit exemptions are not single-route verdicts, so they live as standalone notes: the Hainan 30-day exemption for 61 nationalities, China's 240-hour transit exemption, and the Korea group-tour exemption together with the Jeju policy. Each note is bound to an official source URL and cross-checked. Notes appear beside relevant routes in the query tool, on the display pages and in the AI Q&A, with a decisive covered-or-not line whenever the nationality is known.

7. Export caliber

The export is an .xlsx workbook with two sheets. The data sheet has field names in the first row. The field description sheet states each field's caliber and the snapshot time in UTC. The export is the online data produced through the same code path; there is no separate offline database. A single export has no row cap.

This specification is maintained alongside the code. Any caliber change updates this document and the export's field description sheet before it takes effect.
