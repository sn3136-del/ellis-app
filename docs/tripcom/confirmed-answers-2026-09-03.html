Question 1 · Data accuracy assurance how accuracy is guaranteed in the system

① Who are the "humans" in the Human-verified segment: Ellis's team, outsourced labelers, or Ctrip's operations?

Ellis's own reviewers. One person writes a fix, a different person must approve it, and the system blocks anyone approving their own work. No outsourced labelers. Trip.com operations has the same console, and your edits are stamped "Trip.com operations" on the record.

② For Source-checked, which fields are compared, how long is the freshness window, and is re-verification automatic?

Ellis reopens the official page each record cites and compares the core fields: requirement, fee, currency, stay, validity, entries, documents, processing time, application channel, and the product list. Every record is rechecked automatically at least every 48 hours, and again whenever someone opens an expired one. Nothing waits for a person to remember.

③ How is confidence calculated? How are multi-source conflicts handled? How is a person notified when a low-confidence answer is intercepted?

High: a person verified the full record against a named official page. Medium: an official source with a few blanks. Low: never shown to customers, who see "we are checking this route" instead. When sources disagree, the destination government's page for that exact nationality wins, and each source keeps its own link. Anything the machine cannot settle lands in the correction queue at once, where operators see it and rule on it.

④ How is the 48-hour commitment for policy changes guaranteed at the system level?

Ellis re-reads every record's official page automatically every 48 hours and corrects differences itself, saving the quote. A fix is data, not code, so the next reader gets it immediately and the old answer is gone. Every step is timestamped, so the clock is measured, not promised.

Question 2 · Supplementing missing information

① What is the mechanism for filling gaps like the South Korea zero and the incomplete Australia visa types?

Every blank cell is one of three things: missing, not published by the government, or not applicable. Missing ones are researched against official pages until they close. The South Korea zero was a display filter bug, the data existed; it is fixed with regression tests. Australia's multi-year visas are live. Completeness today is 100%.

② Can Ctrip's operations team edit or add fields on their own, or does it depend on Ellis?

Fully self serve. Add a route two ways: Ellis AI answers it from official pages, or you type the fields yourself. Any field can be edited later. Every record has a "Refresh from source" button that re-reads the official page and says plainly when nothing needs updating. Every change needs an official source link and a short note.

Question 3 · Installation package for localization acceptance

Delivered as a zip with a Docker Compose package. One command starts the backend and the website on your machine, with a database snapshot so acceptance works offline. The README walks through it.

Question 4 · Verifying policy update timeliness in practice

① How do we simulate a policy change and measure the whole process from detection to correction to confirmation to launch?

Pick any route and flag a field in the console: that is T0. Ellis corrects it, a second person reviews, and it publishes. Every stage stamps its own time, so the total reads off the screen. The Freshness tab also shows the next automatic re-check and how much of the database was re-read in the last 48 hours; today that is all of it.

② Is there a change notification mechanism (email or system reminder) for the operations team?

The change log lists every change with before and after values, and open browser tabs show a refresh banner when a new version lands. Email or webhook push is the one item not live yet: tell us the channel and Ellis ships it within one week.
