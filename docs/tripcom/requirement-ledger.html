1. The completeness question 字段完整度口径

This is the single most consequential open item, and it is a definition rather than a defect. The standard defines completeness twice, in two different ways, and the two readings are 48 points apart on the same data.

ELLIS READING
99.09%

Filled ÷ cells that can be filled. Excludes 1,440 cells that cannot apply, because a visa-free route has no visa validity, and 316 that no government publishes, because §4.2.1 forbids inventing them.

§6.1 LITERAL
91.34%

已填字段数 ÷ 必填字段总数. Every required cell in the denominator, whether or not it could ever hold a value. This is the table the standard calls the unified measurement basis.

§4.2.2 LITERAL
44.61%

Records with all required fields non-null ÷ total records. A record-level test: one blank anywhere fails the whole record.

Why the three differ, and what we need from you. §6.1 counts cells; §4.2.2 counts whole records. The standard applies the same ≥99% bar language to a metric it has defined two ways, and the two cannot both be intended, because no visa database of any depth reaches 99% of records with every field non-null. Our reading removes only cells that cannot truthfully hold a value. One point in our favour is settled: the dictionary marks exactly 20 of the 25 fields required, with processing time, consulate district, entry requirements and special conditions marked 如有则提供, and Ellis uses precisely that set of 20. The remaining question is whether a cell that cannot apply belongs in the denominator.

10. What it would take to pass

  • Settle the completeness denominator. One ruling from Party A moves this metric from 91.34% to 99.09% or the reverse. Nothing else we can build changes it as much.
  • Availability needs a clean month, not code. 99.99% allows 4.3 minutes of downtime in thirty days and we have spent three, all self-inflicted. The fix is that deployments stop happening against the instance being measured.
  • The classification tree. Fold Conditional into the three values the dictionary defines, nesting electronic authorizations under 需提前办理, or have Party A accept a fourth. Visible to any reviewer who opens two records.
  • Gate the three required inputs that currently default silently, and put the departure city into the cache key so it can change an answer.
  • 188 cells stand between us and a fuller completeness figure, concentrated in Sudan, North Korea, Syria, Chad, Congo and Niger, where consular fees are genuinely not posted online.
  • Station depth. Sixteen stations at roughly 14 destinations each need to reach what Hong Kong and the United States already have. This is Stage 4's whole subject and the largest remaining body of work.
  • Smaller and each visible in a demo: the console's three missing stage buttons, the deep link that does not re-render, the held page that hides its own switchers, deletion in the change log, and a write path for Q&A sampling verdicts.

11. Questions only Trip.com can settle

  • Which completeness definition governs, §6.1's cell count or §4.2.2's record count, and does a cell that cannot apply belong in the denominator?
  • Is Conditional acceptable as a fourth primary classification, or must electronic authorizations sit under 需提前办理 as the dictionary shows?
  • For info_validity, do you want the policy's own expiry date, left blank where no government publishes one, or the date Ellis warrants the row to and rechecks it? Today it is the latter, declared in the export's field description sheet.
  • Does station coverage mean eighteen present or eighteen at comparable depth? The answer changes the remaining scope by an order of magnitude.
  • Who performs the §4.2.2 accuracy sampling, and against which sample frame?
  • How should cross-validation be evidenced when a record can bind only one URL?
