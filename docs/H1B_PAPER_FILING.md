# H-1B I-129 Paper (Mail) Filing — verified 2026-08-09

Paper filing is fully permitted in 2026 for H-1B extensions and amendments;
online filing is optional, never mandatory. Ellis offers BOTH paths as
first-class: an online-filing prep and a mail-ready paper packet. Re-verify
the lockbox address on uscis.gov/i-129-addresses AT PACKET-GENERATION TIME —
USCIS updates addresses annually (usually February). Sources were read via
search excerpts (uscis.gov 403-blocks direct fetch); revalidate before the
first real mailing.

## Where the packet goes (non-cap H-1B: extension/amendment/transfer)

Since 2024-04-01 all paper I-129 petitions go to a USCIS lockbox, not a
service center. Extensions, amendments, concurrent employment, and
same-employer petitions route to the Dallas lockbox regardless of the
employer's state:

- USPS: USCIS, Attn: I-129, P.O. Box 660867, Dallas, TX 75266
- Courier (FedEx/UPS/DHL): USCIS, Attn: I-129,
  2501 S. State Hwy. 121 Business, Suite 400, Lewisville, TX 75067

Premium filings for extensions/amendments go to the same Dallas lockbox per
law-firm guidance (cap-subject premium uses a distinct box). USCIS sometimes
assigns a separate premium P.O. box — verify on the live I-129 addresses
page immediately before mailing. Sources: uscis.gov/i-129-addresses;
peterchu.com H-1B lockbox guidance. Confidence: high on lockbox, medium on
the premium box.

## Signature rules (paper)

An original handwritten wet-ink signature is required on the form (I-129
Part 7; I-907 if included). Rejected: typed names, stamps, DocuSign-style
e-signatures, and pasted signature images. A scanned/photocopied
reproduction of a genuinely wet-ink-signed original is acceptable, but the
underlying mark must be pen on that form. A USCIS signature rule effective
2026-07-10 hardens enforcement: an invalid signature can be denied with fees
retained and no cure opportunity. Electronic signatures are valid only in
myUSCIS online filing. Sources: rnlawgroup.com (2026 signature rule);
citizenpath.com signature requirements. Confidence: high.

## What paper loses vs online

- No online case dashboard: paper-filed I-129/I-907 cannot be linked to a
  myUSCIS account.
- RFEs arrive and must be answered by mail.
- Slower intake (lockbox mail handling + manual data entry) and paper-only
  notices.
Online gives the dashboard, e-notices, online RFE response, and e-signature.
Confidence: high on the account link, medium on the rest.

## Premium processing with paper

Include Form I-907 (fee + wet-ink signature) in the same package as the
paper I-129, mailed to the same lockbox. No online account is needed.
Sources: uscis.gov premium processing pages. Confidence: high.

## Dependents change the calculus — toward paper

- Concurrent adjudication of H-4 (I-539/I-765) with the I-129 happens only
  when filed together in ONE paper package; the online path splits them into
  separate submissions that are not bundled.
- More than one dependent (I-539 + I-539A supplements) forces paper — online
  I-539 does not support the multi-dependent flow.
- A standalone principal extension with no dependents can stay online.
Sources: rnlawgroup.com H-4 filing strategies; uscis.gov I-539 pages.
Confidence: medium-high.

## Product rules Ellis enforces

1. Offer paper as a first-class path, steering to paper when dependents need
   concurrent filing and to online for standalone principals who want the
   dashboard.
2. Wet-ink discipline: the packet cover names every human-only
   signature/date line; Ellis never prints a signature.
3. The lockbox address on the cover always carries the verify-at-
   uscis.gov/i-129-addresses notice; addresses change annually and the
   values in app/h1b/forms.py are excerpt-sourced, not portal-confirmed.
4. One envelope: I-129 + I-907 (if premium) + dependents' I-539/I-539A (if
   concurrent) + the signed certified LCA + exhibits, in cover-sheet order.
